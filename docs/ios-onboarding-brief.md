# Brief: FocuzNow iOS onboarding, a genuinely new idea

## What FocuzNow is
A focus app for iPhone and iPad (native SwiftUI, iOS 18+, Liquid Glass on iOS 26). It blocks
distracting apps through Apple's Screen Time, runs focus sessions (with a Live Activity on the Lock
Screen and in the Dynamic Island), and has plans and to-dos, a password manager (FocuzPass) and an AI
coach. The audience is students and young adults (the copy talks about school and homework).

## The job
An onboarding as good as Opal's: cinematic, spacious, every element animated, premium and alive,
not a black screen. But it must not look or work like Opal: no gem or orb, no "X years of your
life" stat payoff, no copy of their layouts.

## Must
- It feels like a **story you tap through** ("tap to continue"). Not a puzzle where you drag things
  around, and not the classic screen → question → Continue → next screen form.
- Name, then about four questions: phone time per day, biggest distraction, hardest time of day,
  what they want more time for. **A specific reply for every answer.**
- Then the Screen Time permission, picking apps (Apple's own picker), notifications, an account
  (Apple, Google or email), and optionally starting a first 10-minute session or a daily block.
- Works on **every iPhone and iPad** (not all have a Dynamic Island).
- Follows Apple's rules: no fake system screens (no fake Lock Screen), and apps can't use the
  person's wallpaper.
- Text is always readable; motion is smooth (60–120 fps).
- Buildable by one developer, with art from AI image generation (no animator, no 3D team).

## Already tried and rejected (don't pitch these again)
- Colourful gradients and a "beam" logo: looked AI-made, too pink.
- A blurry "tap into focus" intro with bokeh dots forming a logo: weird, unreadable.
- A chat-style conversation: it flowed well, but it was too close to Opal.
- A Braun-style device, a paper journal, an arcade game: off-brand.
- Pixel or ASCII art; a 3D lighthouse rendered in code: "looks like Tinkercad".
- One question per screen with a reply card and a Continue button: felt like a form.
- Flicking answers into a fake Dynamic Island: not on every phone, felt too big.
- A recreated Lock Screen with notifications: against Apple's rules.
- A floating robot guide in a space scene, with subtitles and glass answer buttons: felt bad.
- Also floated, not picked: putting apps in a box, TV static clearing into a signal, a
  day-in-your-life timeline, a letter from future you.

## What the owner liked
The flow of the chat version, a typed name field with an arrow button, answer chips arriving one
after another, and the idea of a character who guides you.

## Brand
Open. The current website is only a concept (black, bone `#ECE8DF` cards, white buttons, Satoshi
headlines, a lighthouse).

## What to send back
Three genuinely different concepts. For each:
1. The core idea in one sentence, and why it fits a focus app.
2. A beat-by-beat storyboard: what you see, what moves, what you tap.
3. The visual style, with 2–3 real references (apps, games, films, title sequences).
4. What art would need to be generated.
5. The risks.

Then recommend one.

## Already built (stays whatever the idea)
A real Live Activity, real app blocking (sessions and a daily block), and Settings → Developer mode.
